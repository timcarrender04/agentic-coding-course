## Narration

This one's different. No new material, no demo, and — just this once — no help. Lesson seven is the module's phase gate: a fifteen-minute self-test that proves you can drive VS Code without a guide. If you get stuck, you don't ask me and you don't ask your neighbor. You re-read the lesson that covered it. That's the whole point — the lessons are your documentation now.

Here's what the test asks of you, and notice it's everything this module taught, chained together. First, open the command palette with the keyboard shortcut and use it — only it — to toggle the integrated terminal. No menus, no control backtick. Second, install an extension you've never seen: Markdown All in One, publisher Yu Zhang, and verify it lands in your installed section. Third, connect to a remote environment — W S L if you're on Windows, or the college server over S S H for anyone. Confirm the bottom-left of your window goes green. Fourth, open a folder on that remote side. And fifth, in the remote terminal, run two commands: who am I, all one word, and P W D. Both should answer from the remote, not your laptop.

The artifact is one single screenshot that captures two things at once: the green W S L or S S H indicator in the bottom-left, and the terminal showing the output of both commands. One window, one shot, both visible. Submit it in D2L.

You pass the module when that screenshot clearly shows both. If it's missing one, fix it before you submit — don't send a maybe. And if you genuinely can't get there in fifteen minutes, flag me at the end of the time block and we'll regroup.

Most of you will get there, and here's what it buys you: Module one starts with you sitting in front of a working Linux terminal, exactly where this screenshot leaves you. Two and a half hours of typing drills are about to make the terminal feel like home. Get a glass of water, stretch your fingers, and go take the test. I'll see you in Module one.
