# Video: The Integrated Terminal

**Lesson:** [02-integrated-terminal.md](../02-integrated-terminal.md) · **Target:** ~2.5 min · **Words:** 369

## Narration

In the last lesson you found the panel at the bottom of the VS Code window. Today we put something in it: the terminal. These ten minutes matter more than they look, because Module one is two and a half hours of typing commands into this exact panel. Today we're just learning where it is and what it looks like.

Quick refresher. A terminal is a program where you type a command, press Enter, and the computer runs it and prints text back. You already have one on your machine — Command Prompt or PowerShell on Windows, the Terminal app on a Mac. VS Code's integrated terminal is the same kind of program, just embedded inside the IDE so you never have to alt-tab away.

Now, the single most common beginner mistake, and I want you to hear it before you make it. The editor and the terminal look similar — they're both places where you type. But when you type in the editor, you're typing into a file. Nothing runs. When you type in the terminal and press Enter, the computer runs your command. Type a command into the editor and nothing happens. Type a sentence into the terminal and it complains. So before you type anything, look at where your cursor is blinking.

Here's your lab. There are three ways to open the terminal: control backtick — backtick is the key just under Escape, it shares with the tilde — or the View menu, or the command palette. Pick one, open it, and click inside the panel. Then type echo hello and press Enter. Echo just repeats whatever you give it, so hello should print right back at you. Do it again with your own name. Then close the terminal and reopen it — your output is still sitting there.

You're done when echo hello prints hello back at you in the terminal panel, not in a file. Take a screenshot of your VS Code window with the terminal showing both of your echo commands and their output, and submit it in D2L. No commands to memorize today — that's Module one's problem. Open the lesson, find your terminal, and I'll see you in the next one.
